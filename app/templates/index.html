How a label is checked

  1. Read. The label image is read with local OCR (Tesseract). Nothing leaves this server.{% if cloud %} A cloud reader (Claude Vision) can be selected for comparison.{% endif %}
  2. Find each field. For every value on the application we look for the best-matching text on the label and remember where it was read.
  3. Compare. Capitalization, punctuation, spacing and unit spelling are ignored ("Alc./Vol." = "ABV", "750 mL" = "750ml", 90 proof = 45%). Alcohol content and net contents are compared as numbers.
  4. Government warning. The statement must be present, word for word, with "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" in capitals and bold.

What the results mean

  • {{ icon('check') }}MATCH identical after normalization.
  • {{ icon('bang') }}NEAR MATCH the same words with different capitalization (brand name), a value that is only part of what the label says, or at least {{ thresholds.near_match }}% similar. You decide.
  • {{ icon('cross') }}MISMATCH the label says something different from the application.
  • {{ icon('question') }}NOT FOUND nothing on the label resembles the application value.

Government warning checks

  • Present: the statement was found on the label.
  • Wording: compared word for word with the required text; differences are listed. A small difference ({{ thresholds.warning_near }}% similar or more) asks for a look because it may be a reading error; anything bigger fails.
  • Capitals: "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" must be printed in capitals.
  • Bold: estimated from the thickness of the heading's strokes against the rest of the statement (bold from {{ thresholds.bold_ratio }}×). A heuristic, so a doubtful result asks for a look rather than failing the label.

Required text: “{{ warning_text }}”

Good to know

  • Works best with flat, straight-on label images (artwork files or scans). Photos at an angle, with glare or poor lighting are out of scope for this prototype.
  • Images up to 20 MB; batches up to 500 labels. Nothing is stored after the check.
  • Every threshold lives in one configuration file and is documented in the project README.
