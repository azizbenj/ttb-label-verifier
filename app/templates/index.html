How a label is checked

  1. Read. The label image is read with local OCR (Tesseract). Nothing leaves this server. {% if cloud %}A cloud reader (Claude Vision) can be selected for comparison.{% endif %}
  2. Find each field. For every value on the application we look for the best-matching text on the label.
  3. Compare. Capitalization, punctuation, spacing and unit spelling are ignored ("Alc./Vol." = "ABV", "750 mL" = "750ml", 90 proof = 45%). Alcohol content and net contents are compared as numbers.
  4. Government warning. The statement must be present, word for word, with "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" in capitals and bold.

What the results mean

MATCHIdentical after normalization.
NEAR MATCHSame words with different capitalization or punctuation (for the brand name), or at least {{ thresholds.near_match }}% similar. An agent should confirm.
MISMATCHThe label says something different from the application.
NOT FOUNDNothing on the label resembles the application value.

Government warning checks

  • Present: the statement was found on the label.
  • Wording: compared word for word with the required text. Differences are listed. A small difference ({{ thresholds.warning_near }}% similar or more) is flagged for review because it may be a reading error; anything bigger fails.
  • Heading in capitals: "GOVERNMENT WARNING:" must be printed in capitals.
  • Heading bold: estimated by comparing the thickness of the heading's letter strokes with the rest of the statement (bold when at least {{ thresholds.bold_ratio }}× thicker). This is a heuristic, so a doubtful result asks for a look rather than failing the label.

Required text: “{{ warning_text }}”

Good to know

  • Works best with flat, straight-on label images (artwork files or scans). Photos taken at an angle, with glare or poor lighting are out of scope for this prototype.
  • Images up to 20 MB; batches up to 500 labels. Nothing is stored after the check.
  • Every threshold lives in one configuration file and is documented in the project README.
