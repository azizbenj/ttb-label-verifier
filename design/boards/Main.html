Direction

Evidence beside the label.

Every verdict sits next to its proof: the label image is the primary surface, and each field, number and warning check points at the exact place on the label where it was read. A batch becomes a triage queue that puts REVIEW first and clears PASS and FAIL in bulk, so an agent spends attention only where judgment is needed. The look is an internal compliance tool, not a product: U.S. Web Design System conventions (Public Sans, a navy header, USWDS blue for actions, square tags, a quiet identifier strip), with three verdict colours that also differ in lightness and always carry a word and a glyph.

The one idea

Point at the proof.

A generic admin UI shows a tag and asks you to trust it. A COLA examiner needs the proof. Here, hovering or focusing any verdict lights up the words on the label it came from, the numbers show their conversion, and the warning diff is word-level. The chip is never more than one glance from the crop.

Five rules the screens follow

  1. Triage first. REVIEW is the first number you see, FAIL the second, PASS the third. The queue opens on what needs you.
  2. Evidence, not verdict theatre. Crop, conversion, diff, confidence: one glance away, never a modal away.
  3. Nothing is colour-only. Every status carries a word and a glyph; numbered pins tie rows to the image.
  4. Speed you can see. "Checked in 0.9 s" is a feature. Progress reports real counts and a truthful estimate.
  5. The regulation's words. MATCH, NEAR MATCH, MISMATCH, NOT FOUND, PASS, REVIEW, FAIL and the plain-language reasons stay exactly as the agents know them.
